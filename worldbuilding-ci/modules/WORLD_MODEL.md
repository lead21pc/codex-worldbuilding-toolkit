# World Model Module

## Purpose and activation

Use this optional module when the task requires explicit reasoning about entities, actors, institutions, systems, relations, authority, capability, or access. These are reasoning distinctions, not a mandatory schema that every project must adopt.

Apply the [core](../core/GENERIC_WORLDBUILDING_CI_CORE.md) first. This module does not redefine authorship, project-state mutation, evidence, proposal, or language rules.

## Entities, states, mechanisms, and relations

Model relevant entities, states, mechanisms, and typed relations without assuming they form one hierarchy. Preserve meanings declared by the project. Do not merge systems merely because they share vocabulary or surface behavior.

Do not infer:

- containment from interaction;
- dependency or autonomy from co-occurrence;
- authority from power, status, ownership, or membership;
- permanent coupling from temporary cooperation;
- causality from chronology or correlation;
- current hierarchy from genealogy or presentation order;
- universal rules from isolated cases or convention.

An unconfirmed relation remains unknown. Composition is an operation, not automatically an ontology; naming or formalization does not prove origin.

## Actors and information

Actors use only information and memory available to them, together with their beliefs, incentives, authority, abilities, resources, biases, and limits. Do not transfer user knowledge, hidden project state, future events, true motives, system definitions, or author intent without an information path.

Do not infer motives, traits, archetypes, or broad knowledge without evidence.

Do not silently rewrite an established position, motive, doctrine, or relationship. A change requires a supported trigger such as new information, altered incentives, pressure, loss, authority, learning, or coordination. "Actor stupidity" and unexplained universal competence are not mechanisms.

## Capability, authority, and access

Keep distinct:

- capability;
- knowledge;
- access;
- legitimacy;
- jurisdiction;
- mandate;
- control;
- infrastructure;
- resources;
- permission;
- implementation capacity;
- enforcement;
- limits and exceptions.

Possessing one does not grant the others. For action across a system, polity, world, or other material boundary, establish a compatible interaction path.

Distinguish attempt, ability, permission, execution, and effect. Power is not jurisdiction, and a goal is not operational capacity.

## Output boundary

Expose only distinctions that are material to the requested conclusion. Module analysis remains provisional under the core until the user accepts a project-state change.
