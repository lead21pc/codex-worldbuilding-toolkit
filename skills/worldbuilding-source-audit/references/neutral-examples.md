# Neutral examples

These fictional cases demonstrate the audit method. They do not define a source hierarchy for real projects.

## 1. Authority is unresolved

`Atlas.md` says the city of Glass Harbor is governed by a council. `Campaign Notes.txt` says it is governed by a hereditary steward. The project has no authority guide and neither file declares its role.

Result: record both claims and their locations, mark authority unresolved, and do not choose a government model from filename, prose quality, or modification time.

## 2. Apparent conflict across eras

One source states that skyships cannot cross the Ash Meridian before year 410. Another records the opening of a safe route in year 417.

Result: no active contradiction if the dates and change are established. The earlier restriction remains historical evidence for its period.

## 3. Direct evidence versus interpretation

A bestiary states that dusk moths gather near blue flame. A field note concludes that blue flame creates dusk moths.

Result: gathering is direct evidence; creation is a derived causal claim without an established premise. Mark the causal claim unsupported or unknown.

## 4. Unknown is not negation

The checked sources never define whether mirror gates work underwater. A draft says they never do.

Result: unless the draft has declared authority, the project does not yet establish either capability or impossibility. Report the question as unresolved.

## 5. Viewpoint difference

The navigator's journal says no one returned from the western reef. A port registry lists a returning vessel that the navigator could not have observed.

Result: the journal may accurately represent the navigator's knowledge without contradicting the registry's world-level record. Verify viewpoint and scope before declaring a conflict.

## 6. Provisional material

A design board labels three moon names as candidates. A later encyclopedia uses one candidate without documenting approval.

Result: discover the project's adoption rules. The encyclopedia's use may be authoritative, derived, or accidental; do not infer its status from format alone.

## 7. Generated output

An index is generated from several source files and omits an exception present in one input. Project documentation says the index is a navigation aid, not an authority source.

Result: report the index as incomplete for navigation purposes while preserving the input's role. Do not rewrite the world rule to match the generated summary.

## 8. Project-defined invariant

The project guide states that memory transfer always requires direct contact. A new scene depicts remote transfer with no exception or rule change.

Result: cite the invariant and scene, confirm they share scope and continuity, then report a conflict or missing exception. Do not invent an unseen relay mechanism.

## 9. Read-only audit boundary

The user asks whether two histories agree but does not request edits.

Result: report findings and possible resolution choices. Leave both histories unchanged until the user explicitly authorizes a specific modification.
